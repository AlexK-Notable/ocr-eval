As FEMA responded to three major hurricanes on the east coast, on the west, the most destructive wildfires in California's history devastated northern California. During October 2017, wildfires that ignited in California destroyed more than 245,000 acres, an estimated 8,400 structures, and resulted in the loss of 42 lives. In response to the hurricanes and wildfires, the President signed seven major disaster declarations,<sup>2</sup> providing Individual Assistance,<sup>3</sup> Public Assistance,<sup>4</sup> and Hazard Mitigation to affected communities within the designated areas.<sup>5</sup>

---
<sup>2</sup> See table 2 in appendix B for more details of the disaster declarations.
<sup>3</sup> FEMA's Individual Assistance program provides housing assistance and assistance for medical, funeral, and transportation expenses to eligible individuals who, because of a federally declared major disaster or emergency, have necessary expenses and serious needs that insurance or other means does not cover.
<sup>4</sup> FEMA's Public Assistance program awards grants to state, local, and federally recognized tribal governments and certain private non-profit entities to assist them with the response to and recovery from federally declared disasters.
<sup>5</sup> Hazard mitigation is any sustained action taken to reduce or eliminate long-term risk to people and property from natural hazards and their effects.
